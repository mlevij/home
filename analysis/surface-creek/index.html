Key results

Gaged streamflow correlates with peak SWE at r = 0.98 (SURACECO, 2016–2025); total diverted volume correlates at r = 0.81 (all 88 structures). Across the 45 structures with complete 1979–2025 records, large decreed rights track SWE most closely (Spearman ρ = 0.88), small decreed rights show no significant relationship (ρ = 0.06), and junior rights track SWE least closely of the seniority tiers (ρ = 0.32) — see ranking chart.

SURCEDCO's own drainage shows greater runoff efficiency than the area added between the two gages — see Methodology for the terrain and soil comparison. The gaged-plus-diverted ratio exceeds 1 in wetter years from the re-diversion effect noted above.

Water-rights priority & size vs. Park Reservoir SNOTEL snowpack (Spearman ρ, 1979–2025)

Colorado water rights follow prior appropriation: in a dry year, senior rights receive their full decreed amount before junior rights receive any water. Structures were grouped into three tiers by seniority (administration number), decreed quantity, and average annual diverted volume, and each tier's total annual diversion was correlated with Park Reservoir peak SWE. Data: DWR annual diversion records for the 45 of 88 structures with a record in every year 1979–2025 (n = 47 years; 14–15 structures per tier) and Park Reservoir SNOTEL peak SWE. Large and mid decreed rights track SWE closely (ρ = 0.88 and 0.82); small decreed rights show no significant relationship (ρ = 0.06, p = 0.70). By seniority, mid-priority rights track SWE most closely (ρ = 0.76) and junior rights least (ρ = 0.32). Priority and size do not account for total diversions tracking SWE less closely (r = 0.81) than gaged streamflow (r = 0.98). Points show Spearman ρ and lines its 95% confidence interval; blue: p < 0.05, gold: not significant.

Snowmelt-to-streamflow lag (1979–2025)

Monthly snowmelt (the month-over-month decline in SWE) cross-correlated against streamflow at 0–3 month lags. The relationship is strongest in the same month and collapses within a month or two — consistent with this being a small, steep basin with a short time of concentration.

Soil moisture–SWE lag, by depth (Park Reservoir SNOTEL, 2016–2025)

Departure from saturation (each depth's own dataset maximum, treated as a saturation proxy) cross-correlated against SWE at lags of 0–120 days, excluding readings while soil temperature at that depth is at or below 32°F (frozen soil reads as a sharp, artificial VWC drop rather than a real moisture change). Each point pairs soil moisture on day D with SWE on day D − lag. Greater snowpack followed by wetter soil gives a smaller departure from saturation, so a stronger relationship appears as a more negative r, and each line's low point marks the best-fit lag. Accounting for lag strengthens the correlation at every depth (2in: −0.27 to −0.37; 8in: −0.22 to −0.42; 20in: −0.13 to −0.42), and the best-fit lag grows with depth — 33 days at 2in, 45 at 8in, 72 at 20in — consistent with meltwater taking longer to reach deeper soil.

Methodology

Each diversion structure was assigned a position relative to the two gages (upstream of both, between them, or downstream of both) using flowline routing through the USGS 3D Hydrography Program network. Recorded water source reflects legal water-right designation, not necessarily physical location, and was not used for this routing.

Annual values are aggregated by hydrologic year (November 1–October 31), aligning each winter's snow accumulation with the runoff season it produces. Diverted volume reflects DWR's recorded annual figure per structure; a reservoir's fill and a downstream structure's later re-diversion of the same released water are each recorded independently, so total diverted volume exceeds gaged streamflow. Runoff ratios convert gaged and diverted volume to an equivalent depth using each gage's USGS drainage area for direct comparison against SNOTEL precipitation.

Water-rights priority and size effects were evaluated over 1979–2025, the period covered by the SNOTEL station's own record. Not every structure has a diversion record across that full period, so this analysis uses the 45 (of 88) structures with a complete record in every year, comparing the same set of structures from year to year rather than a count that shifts with reporting gaps. Correlations are reported using Pearson, log-transformed Pearson, and Spearman methods. Confidence intervals for Spearman's ρ use the Fisher z-transformation with the Fieller et al. (1957) standard error, 1.06 / √(n − 3), with n the number of years.

SURCEDCO's and SURACECO's contributing drainage areas were delineated from a 30-meter DEM (USGS 3DEP) using D8 flow routing, validated against USGS's published drainage areas for these gages (09143000 and 09143500: 27.4 and 38.5 square miles) to within 2 percent — 27.6 and 39.2 square miles as delineated here. SURCEDCO's own drainage is steeper than the area added between the two gages (median slope 8.1° vs. 6.9°) and includes more shallow-to-bedrock terrain (gSSURGO puts roughly a third of it within 25–127 inches of bedrock, versus no bedrock within the surveyed 152-inch profile for the dominant soils downstream) — both favor faster runoff over infiltration. Soil hydrologic group runs the other way: Group C soils, generally associated with higher runoff potential, actually cover a larger share of the added area (86 percent) than SURCEDCO's own drainage (48 percent), so soil type alone doesn't explain the pattern in the runoff ratio chart.

Soil moisture–SWE lag: readings while soil temperature at a given depth is at or below 32°F are excluded first, since frozen soil produces a sharp, artificial drop in reported volumetric water content rather than a real moisture change. For each SNOTEL soil-moisture depth (2, 8, 20in), a saturation proxy is defined as the maximum volumetric water content observed at that depth across the full 2016–2025 record (unfrozen readings only), and departure from saturation is the daily difference between that proxy and the day's reading. Departure was cross-correlated against SWE at lags from 0 to 120 days in 3-day steps (SWE on day D−lag vs. departure on day D), separately per depth, using Pearson's r as the primary statistic (Spearman's ρ also computed and reported alongside it in the downloadable CSV).
