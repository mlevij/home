SNOTEL, streamflow, and diversion-structure analysis · Delta County, CO

Compares Park Reservoir SNOTEL (snow water equivalent, precipitation) against the two real USGS/DWR stream gages on Surface Creek and the network of 88 DWR-administered diversion structures (reservoirs, ditches, pumps) across the Surface Creek and Fruit Growers Reservoir HUC12s, to see how well upstream diversion activity and streamflow track that year's snowpack.

Key results

Total diverted volume correlates with peak SWE at r = 0.81 (2016–2025, all 88 structures) — SURACECO's own gaged flow correlates more tightly, at r = 0.98. Over the longer 1979–2025 window, large/senior structures track SWE more weakly than small/junior ones — see the ranking chart below.

Study area & diversion structures

SNOTEL (Park Reservoir) — inches

Peak SWE and total water-year precipitation, both in inches — matched units, one chart.

Streamflow & diverted volume — acre-feet

Gaged annual volume at both USGS gages, plus diverted volume from structures actually upstream of each gage (not the full 88-structure sum). Diverted volume runs higher than gaged flow because DWR counts a reservoir's fill and a downstream ditch's later re-diversion of that same released water as two separate diversions — expected, not an error.

Runoff ratio — dimensionless

(Volume ÷ drainage area, converted to inches) ÷ total precipitation. Gaged-only sits in a physically plausible 0.1–0.4 range for a semi-arid mountain watershed; gaged+diverted runs above 1 in wetter years from the same reuse effect noted above, not a real water-balance violation.

Methodology

Diversion structures are tagged as upstream of SURCEDCO, between the two gages, or downstream of SURACECO by their position along Surface Creek and its tributaries, traced through the USGS 3D Hydrography Program flowline network clipped to the Surface Creek and Fruit Growers Reservoir HUC12s. DWR's own recorded water source for a structure doesn't always match the creek it physically sits on, so that field isn't used for this routing — two structures administratively tied to Surface Creek turned out to sit on Milk Creek, confirmed by locating Milk Creek's actual confluence along the flowline network.

Annual comparisons use a hydrologic year running November 1–October 31, matching when diversion activity in this basin is actually dormant (roughly November–March) versus active. Diverted volume is DWR's own recorded annual figure per structure; a reservoir's fill and a downstream ditch's later re-diversion of that same released water are both counted, so diverted volume naturally runs higher than gaged streamflow — an accounting characteristic of the data, not an error in this comparison.

The priority/size ranking chart uses a longer 1979–2025 window (bounded by the Park Reservoir SNOTEL station's own period of record) and a 45-structure subset with a complete diversion record across every one of those years, so each ranking tier sums a consistent set of structures rather than a shifting one. Diverted volume across structures is right-skewed, so results are reported as raw Pearson r, log-transformed Pearson r, and Spearman rank correlation together rather than relying on one measure.
