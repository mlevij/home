## Part 2: Toggle behavior — three iterations to get right

The "CO Soil Health" sidebar toggle went through three designs before landing correctly, each based on a genuine misunderstanding of either the requirement or Leaflet's actual behavior:

1. **First attempt:** CO Soil Health's own toggle gated visibility with AND logic (`SMM on && CO Soil Health on`). Bug: turning off CO Soil Health while trying to view "SMM sites only" hid it, unlike CoAgMET/RAWS/SNOTEL whose SMM markers are immune to their own network toggle.
2. **Second attempt:** moved markers solely onto the shared `soilLayer`, no independent toggle control at all. This satisfied the "immune to own toggle" requirement but user actually wanted CO Soil Health's toggle to have *real* independent power (OR logic) — turning it off alone should still show sites via SMM, and vice versa.
3. **Discovered a real Leaflet gotcha along the way:** `L.LayerGroup` doesn't reference-count. Adding the *same* marker object to two LayerGroups doesn't give OR-visibility — removing *either* group from the map strips the marker outright via that group's `onRemove`, regardless of the other group's state. A plain-array + manual `map.addLayer`/`removeLayer` workaround was built and confirmed working, but ended up unnecessary.
4. **Final, correct design:** realized CoAgMET/RAWS's *existing* dual-marker pattern already solves this cleanly — two genuinely separate marker objects (a subdued "plain dot" via `circ()` on the network's own layer, plus a brighter glowing marker on the shared `soilLayer`), no sharing, no custom logic. This also happened to satisfy a *fourth*, purely aesthetic requirement raised at the very end: CO Soil Health should visually fade (not vanish) when SMM is off but its own toggle is on, matching how the other three networks look. Standard independent toggle wiring just works once the markers are truly separate.

**Takeaway saved to memory:** don't reach for "add one marker to two LayerGroups" as a shortcut for multi-toggle visibility logic on this map again — go straight to two separate marker objects.
